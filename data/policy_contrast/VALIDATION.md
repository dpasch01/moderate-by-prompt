# Validation of the Policy Contrast Set

## Protocol

- Three annotators (a lecturer, a PhD student, and an undergraduate student) judged every text under both policies.
- Texts and policies were presented in randomized order, without expected labels.
- For each text and policy, annotators judged whether the text meets the definition. They also rated whether the text is grammatical and clear.
- **Acceptance rule (frozen before annotation):** a text is accepted if at least two annotators give the expected answer under both policies, and at least two judge it grammatical and clear.

## Results

All 240 texts were accepted. There were no uncertain or missing responses, and all 720 quality ratings were positive.

| Text type | Annotators matching expected under both policies | Texts |
|---|---|---:|
| Hateful, protected under both | 3 of 3 | 90 |
| Hateful, contested group | 2 of 3 | 90 |
| Neutral | 3 of 3 | 31 |
| Neutral | 2 of 3 | 29 |

## Agreement

| Policy | A1–A2 | A1–A3 | A2–A3 | Fleiss κ |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| P_in | 121/240 (κ .01) | 240/240 (κ 1.00) | 121/240 (κ .01) | .26 |
| P_out | 211/240 (κ .76) | 240/240 (κ 1.00) | 211/240 (κ .76) | .83 |

A1 and A3 give the expected answer on all 480 policy judgments. A2 matches the expected label on 121 of 240 texts under P_in and 211 of 240 under P_out.

A2 gives the same answer under both policies for every text:
- Hateful texts about men, white people, or straight people are non-hate under both policies (expected hate under P_in).
- Hateful texts about women, Black people, or gay people are hate under both policies, as expected.
- Of the neutral texts, A2 labels 29 of the 30 that mention women, Black people, or gay people as hate. The other 31 are non-hate.

This pattern is consistent with applying the exclusion sentence under both policies and treating mentions of protected groups as meeting the definition. A2's judgments are reported as observed, not adjudicated or removed. They do not change acceptance, because A1 and A3 agree on every text.
